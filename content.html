Selected projects

Wearable stress detection for neurodevelopmental disordersFlagship

Problem

People with ASD or ADHD can have sudden stress episodes that are hard to spot early. Heart rate and skin conductance often change before behaviour does. A wearable could alert caregivers in time.

Approach

Sensor signals go through filtering and feature extraction into a classifier, with alerts to a mobile app. Tap a stage to see what happens there.

Heart rate/SpO2 (MAX30105), GSR (Grove) and temperature (DS18B20) sensors capture signals on the wrist.

Patent

I have pursued a patent application related to the wearable device. No patent has been granted, and I make no claim about its novelty or patentability.

My contribution

Co-authored the research paper (SRMIST, with Dr. Kavitha R. as supervisor) and worked on the data, model and pipeline design.

Stage 1 — Research paper

78.87%Random Forest accuracy on public HR + EDA data (software simulation; hardware proposed)
3 classesCalm, moderate and high arousal

Stage 2 — Advanced implementation (conference presentation)

88.4%GSR stress classification accuracy
93.6% / 91.2%HR anomaly sensitivity / specificity
320 msend-to-end alert latency
~8 hreported battery operation

Stage 2 figures are as reported in my conference presentation. They are not independently verified clinical results.

Quantum binary classifier for student performance

Problem

Can a variational quantum classifier match a classical baseline when predicting student performance?

Approach

PCA reduces features; angle embedding encodes them into qubits; RY rotations and CNOT entanglement form the circuit, trained with hybrid quantum-classical optimisation (Qiskit / PennyLane). Compared with Logistic Regression.

Results, honestly

~80%Logistic Regression test accuracy
~62–70%VQC across multiple runs

The classical baseline performed better. Quantum did not outperform it here.
